# ADR 0008 — May a league-wide home-field constant be estimated from earlier seasons?

- **Status:** **OPEN — AWAITING THE OWNER'S DECISION.** Nothing has been decided
  and nothing has been changed. This ADR asks a question and lays out the evidence
  for answering it either way. `configs/default.toml` is unchanged on this point
  and stays unchanged until the owner rules.
- **Date:** 2026-08-12
- **Evidence:** [`docs/analysis/campaign-2.md`](../analysis/campaign-2.md) PART 6
  (generated by `scripts/campaign_2.py`; every number is in the sibling `.json`)
- **Protocol:** [`_campaign-2-protocol.md`](../analysis/_campaign-2-protocol.md)
  PART 0.5, committed at `772ee7f` **before any number below was read**. The case
  for and the case against were written there, in full, in advance, so that neither
  could be quietly re-weighted once the number was known.
- **Touches:** [`docs/constraints.md`](../constraints.md) constraint 2 and the

## The question, in one sentence

> **May the home-field advantage `h` — one league-wide scalar, carrying no team
> identity — be estimated from home-and-home pairs in earlier seasons, given that
> `docs/constraints.md` bans "prior-season ratings of any kind"?**

## Why it is being asked

Campaign 1 convicted `h` of being barely identified, and campaign 2 re-measured it
on the current config:

| Estimate | h | n | width |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **The site coefficient that actually runs**, mean over published weeks | **6.230** | 33 weeks | sd **4.424**, range **−3.741 to +14.863** |
| Home-and-home, pooled over 2021-2023 (**not runnable live**) | 1.876 | 1,113 pairs | SE 0.339 |
| Home-and-home, within season (the only form constraint 2 allows) | −0.143 | 21 pairs | SE 2.377 |
| `[homefield].h_pasteur` (inherited reference) | 3.70 | — | — |
| `[homefield].h_recent_estimate` (inherited reference) | 2.80 | — | — |

Every credible published estimate of home-field advantage in college football is
between 2 and 3 points. The number this poll runs on averages 6.23, and in some
published weeks it is **negative** and in others it is nearly **fifteen points**.
Only 37 of the 1,585 scored games are at neutral sites, so in the affine points
calibration the intercept and the site column are very nearly collinear and `h` is
not identified in any meaningful sense. That is a real defect, it is on the record
since campaign 1, and nothing has been done about it.

The one identification strategy anyone has proposed uses prior-season home-and-home
pairs. Hence the question.

## THE CORRECTION THAT HAS TO COME FIRST

**It was said, in the course of setting campaign 2 up, that anchoring `h` on prior
seasons would extend an existing precedent — that the EP layer already fits across
seasons. That is false, and the record should say so plainly rather than let a
convenient premise stand.**

`[ep].fit_scope = "training_window"`: the expected-points model is fitted inside
the walk-forward window like everything else. `frozen_seasons` is an alternative
the config names and **no code path selects**. The L3 blend weights are pooled over
one season and never across seasons, and `backtest/walkforward.py` says so at the
line that does it: *"pooling across seasons inside a walk-forward run would carry
information from season S-1 into season S's weights, and constraint 2 forbids prior
seasons reaching anything the model uses."* Cross-season fitting was raised earlier
in this project's life and rejected as leakage.

**Every fitted quantity in this system is currently within-season and
walk-forward. Anchoring `h` would be the FIRST cross-season fitted quantity.** Not
an extension. A first. `tests/test_config.py` now asserts this so the claim cannot
quietly stop being true.

## The case FOR

- **`h` is not a rating and carries no team identity.** Constraint 2's banned list
  says "prior-season *ratings* of any kind", and `docs/constraints.md` explains at
  length what the ban is *for*: a reputation prior "shrinks a team toward what we
  think of its brand". A single league-wide venue constant shrinks nobody toward
  anything. It is one scalar, identical for all 133 teams, and it cannot advantage
  a brand because it does not know which team it is attached to. The line the
  document draws so it can be audited is *"priors may encode ignorance, never
  reputation"*, and a league-average home-field advantage is closer to a physical
  constant of the sport than to an opinion about anybody.
- **The thing it would replace is worse.** A coefficient with a standard deviation
  of 4.42 points, driven by 37 neutral-site games, is not a measurement of
  home-field advantage. Refusing a 1.876 ± 0.339 in favour of a 6.230 ± 4.424
  protects the letter of the constraint at the cost of the thing the constraint is
  for.
- **It is auditable in exactly the way constraint 5 demands.** One number,
  published weekly, computed by `l2_results.home_and_home_estimate` on the public
  archive, with a standard error beside it.

## The case AGAINST

- **The bright line is the asset.** This project can say "we do not use conference
  identity" as a *result* rather than a claim because `audit-features` rebuilds
  every design matrix from an allow-list and requires bit-identity. A bright line
  survives because it is bright. *"No prior-season data except this one scalar,
  which we judged to be different in kind"* is a line that needs a judgement call
  at every future crossing, and the second crossing is always easier to argue than
  the first.
- **The slope is real, not rhetorical.** Every subsequent cross-season quantity
  will cite this one. A per-conference home-field term is "just a few more
  scalars". A venue-altitude term is "a physical constant of the sport". The
  argument that admits `h` admits those, and this project has no written principle
  today that separates them.
- **Within-season alternatives are not exhausted.** Penalising `h` toward a
  published reference, pooling neutral-site games differently, or reporting the
  identification failure and refusing to act are all available and none of them
  crosses the line.
- **And now, the strongest one: the measured gain is inside the noise.** See below.

## What the experiment found

Run under pre-registration, walk-forward across seasons: for each season, `h` is
pooled over every archived season **strictly before** it. 2025 was not read.

| Season | Prior seasons pooled | h | n pairs | SE |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2021 | **none** | — | 0 | — |
| 2022 | 2021 | **−0.441** | 17 | 2.619 |
| 2023 | 2021, 2022 | 1.681 | 866 | 0.392 |
| 2024 | 2021, 2022, 2023 | 1.876 | 1,113 | 0.339 |

**Two of the four seasons are boundary cases and the protocol predicted both.**
2021 has no prior season in the archive and keeps the fitted coefficient. 2022's
only prior is 2021, which supplies 17 pairs and an estimate of **−0.441** — a
*negative* home-field advantage, which is not a plausible value of the quantity. So
on the tune seasons the arm is: one season untreated, one season treated with
nonsense, one season treated properly.

| Arm | Seasons | MAE | RMSE | SU % | Brier | Max calib. dev. | Violations | Slope |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| incumbent | 2021-2023 | 13.0102 | 16.5472 | 69.27 | 0.19796 | 11.28 pp | 0.2015 | 1.1492 |
| **anchored (arm A)** | 2021-2023 | **12.9871** | 16.5466 | 69.27 | 0.19764 | 12.34 pp | 0.2015 | 1.1571 |
| pooled-h bound (arm B, **NOT RUNNABLE**) | 2021-2023 | 12.9898 | 16.5380 | 69.59 | 0.19756 | 12.37 pp | 0.2015 | 1.1610 |
| incumbent | 2024 | 12.9096 | 16.3216 | 67.74 | 0.20361 | 15.82 pp | 0.1942 | 1.2428 |
| **anchored (arm A)** | 2024 | **12.8949** | 16.3083 | 67.20 | 0.20290 | 12.94 pp | 0.1942 | 1.2513 |

Arm B reads the seasons it is scoring — it is the same device as campaign 1's
oracle σ row, a **bound** on what a perfectly identified `h` could be worth, and it
is not a proposal.

### Read the arithmetic before the argument

- **MAE moves −0.023 on tune and −0.015 on 2024.** The noise floor this project has
  used for three campaigns, and which was reused in campaign 2's protocol before
  any of this was computed, is **0.055**. Both moves are inside it, and the
  *unrunnable* arm B is inside it too, so this is not a matter of the estimator
  being crippled by its boundary cases.
- **Calibration gets slightly worse on tune** (11.28 → 12.34 pp) and better on 2024
  (15.82 → 12.94 pp). It does not hold direction.
- **Retrodictive violations do not move at all**, to four decimals, on either
  season set.
- **The under-dispersion is untouched.** The slope of actual on predicted margin
  goes 1.1492 → 1.1571 on tune and 1.2428 → 1.2513 on 2024 — the wrong way, and by
  nothing. Anchoring `h` is not a fix for the thing that was actually wrong; ADR
  0009 is.
- **The 2022 season is the tell.** It was anchored at a *negative* home-field
  advantage, and the three-season totals still barely moved. An input that can be
  wrong by six points and change almost nothing is not an input the poll is
  standing on.

## The recommendation this ADR makes, and does not act on

**On the evidence: not worth it.** The measured gain is inside a noise floor
declared in advance, it does not hold direction on the calibration criterion, it
moves violations not at all, and it does not touch the defect that campaign 2
actually closed. Crossing the project's only bright line — and being the first
crossing, which sets the precedent for every future one — to buy 0.02 points of MAE
is a bad trade at the price the project charges for it.

**That is a recommendation, not a decision, and it is deliberately separated from
the argument above.** The case for and the case against were written before the
numbers and are not re-weighted by them; what the numbers decide is whether the
question is worth the owner's time, not which side of it is right. If the owner
judges that a poll running on `h = 6.23 ± 4.42` is unacceptable *on its face* —
independently of whether fixing it improves any metric — that is a coherent
position and this evidence does not refute it. Honesty about a badly identified
parameter is itself a product of this project.

### What the owner is being asked to choose between

1. **Ratify.** `[constraints].allow_prior_season_data` becomes `true` *for a
   league-wide venue constant only*, `docs/constraints.md` gains an explicit carve-
   out with a written principle for what else may follow, and `[homefield].anchor_*`
   is switched on. **The carve-out must come with the principle**, or the next
   crossing has no line to argue against.
2. **Refuse, and say why in the constraints document.** `h` stays as it is, the
   6.23 ± 4.42 stays published as the defect it is, and constraint 2 keeps a clean
   edge. This is what happens by default if this ADR is never answered.
3. **Refuse, and fund the within-season alternative.** Penalise `h` toward a
   published reference inside the walk-forward window, which is a shrinkage prior
   encoding ignorance rather than reputation, and is therefore *already* allowed by
   the line as written. Not attempted here; it was not in the pre-registered
   protocol and adding it after seeing these numbers would be the failure the
   protocol exists to prevent.

## What has already been built, and what it does today

- `l2_results.home_and_home_estimate` — the estimator, with the standard error the
  config never carried, unit-tested against the `estimate_home_field` that is
  already published weekly.
- `walkforward.homefield_anchor` and `_fit_affine(fixed_site=...)` — the anchor,
  which holds `h` and fits the intercept and slope on `margin − h·site`.
- **`walkforward.PriorSeasonLocked` — the lock, which is exercised rather than
  described.** A config declaring prior-season provenance raises while
  `[constraints].allow_prior_season_data` is `false`, and it is `false`.
  `tests/test_config.py::test_the_home_field_anchor_is_off_and_the_constraint_that_holds_it_off_is_on`
  fails if anyone switches it on, so whoever does has to answer this ADR in an ADR
  rather than in a diff.

The only place in the repository that flips that constraint key is
`scripts/campaign_2.py`'s `anchor` override, for the duration of one experiment.

## Consequences of leaving this open

1. **The poll keeps running on `h = 6.23 ± 4.42`**, and keeps saying so.
   `docs/methodology.md` and `configs/default.toml` both carry the number and its
   width.
2. **`[homefield].method` still selects nothing.** It names the estimator the
   project would use if the constraint allowed it, and now the estimator exists and
   is tested — so the gap is a policy gap rather than an implementation gap, which
   is a better place for it to be.
3. **Nothing about the other four constraints is in question.** No human polls, no
   reputation priors *about teams*, mandatory opponent adjustment, retroactive
   re-ranking and full transparency are untouched by this ADR, and a ratification
   would not weaken any of them.
