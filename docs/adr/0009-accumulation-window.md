## Context

ADR 0007 ended with four named next items and this campaign took three of them.
It also ended with a diagnosis it deliberately refused to act on:

> The mechanism is named in the campaign document: both the affine points
> calibration and σ are fitted on the games *accumulated so far* in a season, and
> the ratings feeding them improve as the season goes on… **The defect is the
> *shape* of the accumulation window. A trailing window is out of sample too, and
> it is the next campaign's first pre-registered item.**

Campaign 1 was right to refuse. A fix discovered while looking, adopted in the same
breath, is exactly what a protocol exists to prevent. So it was written down first
and then run.

## Decision

**`[resume].sigma_trailing_buckets` = 3 and
`[backtest].calibration_trailing_buckets` = 5. `[margin].c` and `[margin].beta_w`
do not move. Nothing else in the config changes.**

| Parameter | Was | Now | Why it moved, or did not |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[resume].sigma_trailing_buckets` | 0 (cumulative) | **3** | Lead 2's winner. σ over the published window falls 18.46 → 17.12 against a realised RMSE of 16.55. |
| `[backtest].calibration_trailing_buckets` | 0 (cumulative) | **5** | Lead 2's winner, and the key that fixed the under-dispersion. |
| `[margin].c` | 32.0 | 32.0 | Lead 1's winner (`inf`) cleared its own rule and was **blocked by the pre-registered interaction clause**. See below. |
| `[margin].beta_w` | 7.0 | 7.0 | Same. |
| `[margin].c_grid`, `[margin].beta_w_grid` | campaign 1's ranges | **widened** | A record of the search that was actually run. `c_grid` now ends at `inf`. |
| `[homefield].anchor_*` | — | **added, and off** | ADR 0008's experiment needed a code path. The default does not use it and a test asserts that it does not. |
| Everything else | — | unchanged | Not in the pre-registered search space. |

`config_version` moves to `0.2.0`.

| Slope of actual on predicted margin | Tune 2021-2023 | 2024 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Incumbent | 1.1492 ± 0.0437 (**3.4 σ above 1**) | 1.2428 ± 0.0846 (**2.9 σ above 1**) |

**The under-dispersion is gone.** A slope 3.4 standard errors above one becomes a
slope 0.8 standard errors above one, which is indistinguishable from one, and it
replicates on 2024 where the incumbent was *worse* (1.243) than on tune. The
intercept halves on both. Campaign 1 named the mechanism and this is the mechanism
being switched off.

**The gate still fails all five criteria.** MAE and RMSE were never what this
instrument moves; the calibration criterion goes from missing by 6.28 pp to missing
by 2.37 pp against a 5.0 pp threshold, which is the largest single movement any
campaign has made on any gate criterion, and it is still a FAIL.

## The costs, printed rather than buried

1. **Tune MAE rises 0.028 points and 2024 MAE rises 0.094.** The rule fixed in
   advance allowed tune MAE to rise up to 0.055 — ADR 0006's noise floor, reused
   for the third campaign running — and it did not guard 2024 MAE at all, because
   lead 2's objective was calibration and was declared to be calibration before any
   number was read. **The 2024 MAE rise is outside the tune-season noise floor and
   is published here as a cost that the rule did not price.** A future campaign
   that wants MAE back knows where it went.
2. **Straight-up accuracy falls 0.56 pp on tune** — nine games in 1,585 — **and
   rises 0.72 pp on 2024.** Two results in opposite directions on a metric that
   moves by three games at a time; neither is evidence.
3. **Brier improves on both**, by 0.00083 on tune against a paired standard error
   of 0.00078, and by 0.00163 on 2024. The probability forecasts got better, which
   is what a calibration fix ought to do and is not guaranteed by one.
4. **Retrodictive violations do not move at all**, to four decimal places, on
   either season set.

## The uncomfortable result: lead 1 won and was blocked

Campaign 1 left `C = 32` sitting on the top of its own grid, unbracketed. Campaign
2 widened the grid to end at `c = inf` — the identity response, the **limit** of
the family, so the grid cannot produce another corner in `C` — and searched
`β_w` up to 12.

**The optimum is `c = inf`, `β_w = 12`: this dataset does not want the tanh at
all,** and it wants a win premium nearly twice the largest value any precedent
supports. On its own rule it passed cleanly: tune MAE −0.0063, and 2024 MAE
**−0.0560**, the largest single MAE improvement any campaign in this project has
produced.

It is not adopted, for a reason fixed in the protocol before any of it was known:

> If both clear their rules, the joint cell is evaluated on tune and on 2024 and
> must clear **both** rules again before both are adopted together. If the joint
> cell fails, only the lead with the larger pre-declared claim on its own objective
> is adopted, and the other is reported as blocked by the interaction.

The joint cell (`c = inf`, `β_w = 12`, trailing 3/5) makes MAE **worse** than the
incumbent on both tune (+0.019) and 2024 (+0.044), so it fails lead 1's rule while
passing lead 2's. The two leads do not compose.

**The clause that breaks the tie was ambiguous as written, and that is the
protocol author's fault rather than the data's.** "The larger pre-declared claim on
its own objective" compares points of MAE with percentage points of decile
deviation, and this project has no exchange rate between them anywhere. It is
resolved by the only scale-free comparison available — **each lead's claim divided
by the bar it had to clear**, both numbers fixed in advance and neither chosen with
this tie-break in mind:

| Lead | Objective | Claim | Its bar | Claim ÷ bar |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | tune MAE | 0.0063 points | 0.055 (noise floor) | **0.11×** |
| 2 | tune calibration | 3.91 pp | 2.0 pp | **1.96×** |

Lead 2 carries the larger relative claim by a factor of seventeen. Lead 1 is
**blocked by the interaction**, and the arithmetic is published so a reader can
disagree with the resolution without re-deriving it.

**Two things worth saying that the rule did not depend on**, both of them
after-the-fact observations rather than reasons:

- Lead 1 makes calibration dramatically **worse** — 11.28 pp → 16.33 pp on tune —
  and pushes the slope the wrong way, 1.149 → 1.156. Its rule did not guard either.
  The interaction clause happened to catch what an MAE-only rule would have let
  through, and this ADR does not claim that was the design.
- Lead 1's Kendall's τ against the incumbent poll bottoms out at **0.9838**, below
  the 0.985 that ADR 0006 fixed as the line between a convention and a **dial**.
  Adopting it would have been a labelled change to what the poll says about desert,
  bought with 0.006 points of MAE.

## What the adopted change does to the poll

The objective was calibration; the poll is not calibration. Measured with the
project's own standard (headline-ordering study §9, ADR 0006), final
pre-postseason headline poll, incumbent versus adopted:

| Season | Kendall's τ | Mean \|Δrank\| | Max \|Δrank\| | Top-25 changes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2021 | 0.9912 | 0.51 | 5 | 1 |
| 2022 | 0.9890 | 0.64 | 4 | 0 |
| 2023 | 0.9868 | 0.75 | 3 | 0 |

**Minimum τ 0.9868, above the 0.985 floor**, so by the project's published rule
this is a convention rather than a dial. That is a measurement and not an
assurance, which is why the table is published rather than summarised — and it is
worth noting that σ is not merely a scoring quantity: it is the denominator of
every win probability the headline key is a product over, so a change to it moves
the poll and had to be measured doing it.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Adopt lead 1 as well, since it passed its own rule and 2024 loved it.** The
  interaction clause was written precisely so that two leads searched independently
  could not both be adopted on the strength of results neither was measured
  alongside. Waiving it at the moment it produces an inconvenient answer is the
  failure the protocol exists to prevent. Lead 1's result is published in full and
  is the first item any successor campaign should re-open — **pre-registered
  first**, and this time with a calibration guard and a τ disclosure in the rule
  rather than beside it.
- **Adopt lead 1 instead of lead 2, because MAE is the project's usual
  objective.** Lead 2's objective was declared to be calibration before any number
  was read, precisely because campaign 1 had already established that MAE is not
  what this instrument moves. Switching the comparison to the metric that favours
  the other arm, after the fact, is the same failure in the other direction.
- **Take the best trailing cell on MAE instead (σ all / calibration 3, which
  improves MAE to 13.0013 and calibration to 9.68 pp).** That is choosing the cell
  off a scoreboard the lead was not being judged on. The objective was fixed; the
  full 25-cell grid is published so anyone can see that cell and argue for it in a
  campaign of their own.
- **Re-tune C and β_w now that the accumulation window has moved.** The optimum of
  a re-search conditioned on this campaign's own adopted value is a result that
  cannot be validated against anything. Next campaign, pre-registered.
- **Report the improved calibration as passing the criterion.** It is 7.37 pp
  against a 5.0 pp threshold. The gate says what it says.

## Consequences

1. **The gate still fails all five decidable criteria**, and this ADR does not
   change a verdict. It moves one criterion further than any previous change and
   the criterion still fails.
2. **`config_version` → `0.2.0`**, and two more keys move from the config header's
   *inherited* list to its *measured here* list.
3. **The demos and `demo/backtest-2021-2023.json` are regenerated**, so the
   published gate object and the campaign document are the same object. The
   campaign document checks this rather than asserting it.
4. **2024 has now been read twice.** ADR 0007 required that every decision reading
   it again say so publicly and re-designate the split; the campaign document's
   PART 4 and this line are that statement. **2025 remains untouched** and the
   harness still refuses it.
5. **`[garbage_time].mode = "leverage"` has still never been measured**, for the
   second campaign running. It needs a win-probability model, which is a build
   rather than a search.
6. **The next campaign's items, named here before they are searched:** re-open
   `C` and `β_w` on top of the adopted window, with a calibration guard written
   into the rule; a trailing window for the L3 blend weights, which this campaign
   deliberately left cumulative because it was not what campaign 1 named; the
   win-probability model; and whatever ADR 0008 is answered with.
