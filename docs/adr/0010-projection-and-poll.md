# ADR 0010 — The Projection is a second product, and it may never touch the Poll

- **Status:** **ACCEPTED.** Decided by the project owner, whose directive is
  quoted in full below. Implemented in `src/cfbpoll/projection/`, enforced by
  `src/cfbpoll/validate/leakage.py`, and published as
  [`demo/2026-preseason-projection.md`](../../demo/2026-preseason-projection.md).
- **Date:** 2026-08-15
- **Touches:** [`docs/constraints.md`](../constraints.md) — **by adding a second
  product beside the Poll, and by changing nothing at all about the Poll.** All
  five constraints are unchanged, the banned-input table is unchanged, and the
  allow-list rebuild that proves it runs before every poll is fitted, exactly as
  it did yesterday.
- **Evidence:** [`demo/projection-backtest.md`](../../demo/projection-backtest.md)
  (generated by `scripts/make_projection.py`; every number is in the sibling
  `.json`), and
  [`demo/projection-grading-loop.md`](../../demo/projection-grading-loop.md).

---

## 1. The two questions this ADR answers

1. **May this project publish a preseason ranking at all**, given that
   constraint 2 bans returning production, prior-season ratings and coaching
   tenure — which are precisely the only things anyone has in August?
2. **May the 2026 Projection read 2025's fitted ratings**, given that 2025 is
   the sealed single-shot holdout?

The answers are yes and yes, and both rest on the same move: **naming the act
precisely enough that the permission does not generalise.**

## 2. The Projection is not the Poll, and separation is the whole of the argument

The owner's directive, near-verbatim:

> a right-now 2026 preseason top 25 built from how 2025 went on the field plus
> every knowable offseason change, published as "a rough preseason design that
> gets better with time," where week 1 has a poll, and when week 5 shows the
> projection was garbage, the retro machinery re-ranks and SHOWS THE STORY: "we
> thought this, here's what we now know, here's which offseason assumption was
> wrong."

The constraints document bans reputation priors **from the published rankings**.
It is the Poll's charter and it says what the Poll may not do. It does not say
this project may never make a prediction; it says a prediction may never be
sold as a measurement of what was earned.

So there are now two products, and the whole design is the wall between them:

| | **THE POLL** | **THE PROJECTION** |
|---|---|---|
| ranks | what a team **has done** | what we **guess** a team will do |
| from | on-field results only, this season only | last season's fitted ratings + offseason changes |
| headline key | schedule odds, `−log10 P(W ≥ W_t)` | projected Power |
| starts | week 5, a date published in advance | August, before anyone plays |
| may read the other | **never** | yes — the Projection is graded by the Poll |
| governed by | `docs/constraints.md`, all five | this ADR |

**The Projection reads the Poll. The Poll may never read the Projection.**

### Why this is not constraint 2 with extra steps

Three properties, and each is checkable rather than asserted:

1. **It is labelled in every rendered sentence, not only in a header.** A line
   that gets screenshotted has to carry its own label, so the artifact says "this
   is a projection" in the title, in a blockquote, in the JSON's `label` field,
   and inside each generated grading sentence.
2. **It is frozen.** The projection ships once and is never edited, re-run or
   quietly improved. A projection that drifts cannot be graded, and grading it is
   the entire product.
3. **It is graded in public against the poll it cannot influence.** Weekly, from
   week 5, with per-team rank deltas and a league-level attribution of the error
   to the recipe's own terms.

### The enforcement, which is mechanical

`validate/leakage.py` now audits two products with two deny-lists.

- Every column the projection package produces — `returning_*`, `portal_*`,
  `coach_*`, `prior_power*`, `projected_*` — is in `PROJECTION_INPUT_PATTERNS`,
  which is spliced into `BANNED_COLUMN_PATTERNS`. For a **poll** layer, one of
  these being **present in the frame is a violation**, with no consumption test
  required.
- That asymmetry against every other banned pattern is earned by provenance, and
  it is the sharpest thing in this ADR. `excitement_index` is in the games frame
  on every run and always will be, because ESPN shipped it in the same file as
  the facts; the audit's job there is to prove it was never consumed. A
  projection input can only be in a poll frame **because somebody in this
  repository put it there**. There is no innocent explanation, so there is no
  innocent verdict.
- The projection layer is judged against `PROJECTION_BANNED_PATTERNS` instead,
  which allows those four families and still bans **human polls** and
  **third-party fitted models**.

`tests/unit/test_projection_separation.py` plants a projection input in a poll
design matrix and asserts the audit names it, and plants a poll input in the
projection design matrix and asserts the same.

### What the Projection may not use either

This is the line that shows the separation is a design rather than an excuse.

- **Human polls.** The AP preseason top 25 is this product's headline
  **baseline** — the thing it is trying to beat — and a baseline that is also an
  input measures nothing.
- **Third-party fitted models.** SP+, FPI, Elo, CFBD's CORE, and CFBD's PPA. This
  one is load-bearing rather than decorative: CFBD's returning-production endpoint
  serves both `usage` (a counting-stat share — snaps, carries, targets) and
  `percentPPA` (their proprietary model evaluated on their data). Both ride along
  on the same frame. The recipe uses `usage`, and the deny-list is what makes
  that a **fact about the code** rather than a matter of anyone remembering.
- **Recruiting composites.** Not banned by the audit — the portal endpoint's
  `stars` field is right there and a star-weighted net flow would very likely
  predict better. It is refused anyway, in the recipe, on the record. A recruiting
  ranking is the first input constraint 2 names, and using one here would make the
  Poll's refusal look like a technicality.

## 3. The holdout: reading 2025 is not scoring 2025

2025 is `[backtest].holdout_seasons` with `holdout_locked = true`. The 2026
Projection has to regress toward *something*, and the only prior season is 2025.

**A holdout protects one thing: the integrity of hyperparameter tuning.** Its
promise is "no number in `configs/default.toml` was chosen after seeing 2025."
Two acts have to be told apart:

| | act | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **SCORING** | run the walk-forward harness over 2025 and read MAE / SU / Brier / calibration off it | **STILL LOCKED.** `walkforward.HoldoutLocked`, untouched by this ADR, and no code path in this repository passes `unlock_holdout` |
| **READING FITTED RATINGS** | take the L3 Power ratings 2025 produced and use them as an input to a different product, whose coefficients are frozen before 2025 is opened | **SANCTIONED**, via the single named path `[projection].projection_source_season` |

Metric-reading is what a human then tunes against, and it is what burns the
single shot. Nothing about 2025 selects a constant here.

### The test that keeps the permission narrow

`projection/holdout.assert_no_target_is_locked` raises the backtest's **own**
`HoldoutLocked` — deliberately the same exception type, because it is the same
breach — if any design transition's **target** season is locked. The recipe may
**read** a locked season and may never **fit on** one. The 2024→2025 transition
is therefore deliberately absent from `design_transitions` even though the data
for it exists, because its target is 2025.

### The honesty clause, which is the important one

The recipe published for 2026 is designed on **2021→2022, 2022→2023 and
2023→2024**. **2025 is not among the target seasons and the recipe is not
validated on 2025 either.** Anyone extending this must keep that true or must
change this paragraph, in public, in the same commit — because "we tuned nothing
on 2025" and "we checked ourselves against 2025" are different claims and only
the first is being made.

For the same reason, the published grading-loop demo is worked on **2024, not
2025**: grading *is* scoring.

## 4. What the recipe is, and what it is not

Four terms and an intercept, fitted by OLS, every coefficient published:

```
P_hat = intercept
      + phi   * (last season's Power − that season's FBS mean)
      + b_rp  * (returning offensive usage share − mean)
      + b_hc  * (new head coach)
      + b_pf  * (net portal flow, standardised within the season)
```

It is deliberately not more than that. This is v1 of a product whose value is the
grading loop, and a recipe a reader cannot hold in their head cannot be argued
with in public.

**The Projection ranks Power; the Poll ranks schedule odds.** They are different
quantities — schedule odds is a function of the record and cannot exist before
anyone has played — so every comparison between them is a comparison of
*orderings*, never of values.

### What the data cannot do, recorded here because it will still be true next year

- **Returning production is offence only.** CFBD serves no defensive returning
  production of any kind. The offence/defence split the design called for is half
  built, and the half that exists is the offensive half.
- **The portal is the weak data.** `origin` is populated on every row;
  `destination` on 60% of the 2022 cycle and 78% of the 2026 one. Departures are
  measured well and arrivals are not, and the drift across cycles is why the term
  is standardised within each season rather than pooled as a raw count.
- **The coaching term is a binary, not a judgement.** One coefficient for every
  school that changed. No coach is credited by name, and there is no tenure term
  — which is exactly where FPI's version of this becomes a reputation prior.
- **`net_portal` is not statistically distinguishable from zero** on the
  published fit, and it is shipped at its fitted value rather than dropped so the
  grading loop can report, season by season, whether it ever earns its place. A
  term kept at a value the data cannot support is a term on probation, and the
  artifact says which ones those are.

## 5. The result, reported by the party that lost

Out of sample, by leave-one-transition-out:

| system | top-25 hits /25 | rank MAE (censored) |
|---|---:|---:|
| `ap_preseason` | **14.7** | **8.07** |
| `projection` | 14.3 | 8.33 |
| `naive_carryover` | 13.3 | 8.81 |

**We do not beat the AP writers at ranking the season that followed.** We beat
the naive floor — carrying last season's rating forward unchanged — by about one
team and half a place, and that gap is the entire measured value of the offseason
data, because mean reversion is a positive affine map and cannot reorder
anything. We beat the AP at predicting September's games (71.3% straight-up
against 69.0%), which is a different question and is not offered as consolation.

Three transitions is not many, and a 0.3-team difference over three seasons is
inside the noise. **That is the argument for the grading loop rather than against
the product:** this table only becomes evidence after several more seasons are
added to it, in public, without the recipe being quietly re-tuned in between.

## 6. Consequences

- `configs/default.toml` gains a `[projection]` block. Every key in it is banned
  from every poll layer, mechanically.
- `docs/constraints.md` is **unchanged**. The five constraints are the Poll's
  charter and the Poll did not change.
- The Projection carries its own version, `PROJECTION_VERSION`. Changing the
  recipe's terms must bump it: the grading loop is season-over-season and a
  silent recipe change would make the whole record meaningless.
- The weekly poll workflow does not gain a dependency. The projection layers are
  appended to the audit only when a projection design is handed in, so a machine
  that has never computed a projection gets exactly the report it always got.

## 7. What would reverse this

- If the separation is ever found to be leaky in production — a projection input
  reaching a poll fit — the Projection is withdrawn, not patched. The Poll is the
  product with the promise on it.
- If, after five or six more seasons in the loop, the recipe still cannot beat
  the naive carry-over floor by more than noise, the honest move is to publish
  that finding and stop publishing the ranking. A projection that adds nothing to
  "last year's ratings, unchanged" should say so and get out of the way.
