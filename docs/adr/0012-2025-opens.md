# ADR 0012 — 2025 is scored, published and open; the ban moves from a season to an act

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-15
- **Evidence:** [`demo/2025-holdout-scorecard.md`](../../demo/2025-holdout-scorecard.md)
  (generated by `scripts/make_holdout_scorecard.py`; every number is in the sibling `.json`)
- **Provenance:** the constants were frozen at
  [ADR 0007](./0007-tuned-constants.md) and [ADR 0009](./0009-accumulation-window.md),
  both dated **2026-08-12**. 2025 was scored for the first time on **2026-08-15**,
  three days later, by one command logged with its git sha in
  `.cache/holdout-2025.log`. **No number in `configs/default.toml` was chosen after
  any 2025 metric was read**, and this ADR contains no constant change of any kind.
- **Follows:** [ADR 0010](./0010-projection-and-poll.md), which argued the narrow
  version of this distinction while 2025 was still sealed.
- **Numbered after [ADR 0011](./0011-recipes.md)**, which took 0011 for the recipes
  contract on 2026-08-15. The brief for this work said "ADR 0011"; the number was
  already spent by the time it was written, and renumbering a published ADR to
  free a number is worse than saying so here.

---

## Context

`[backtest]` said this, and had said it since the project's first week:

```toml
holdout_seasons = [2025]                    # SINGLE SHOT. Touch it once.
holdout_locked = true                       # challenge.yml refuses to score on it
```

Two things were true at once and only one of them was intended.

**Intended:** no constant in this repository may be chosen after seeing 2025. That
is what a holdout is for, it is the only promise it makes, and it was kept. Two
tuning campaigns ran on 2021-2023, validated twice on 2024, announced both
readings of 2024 in public, and never touched 2025. The independent review named
the lock as one of the nine things this project does that public ranking systems
almost never do.

**Not intended, and inherited by accident:** the season could not be *shown*.
`walkforward.HoldoutLocked` refuses to score it, so no gate verdict existed for
it, so no fixture set could carry one, so the site's example season was 2023 — a
season three years stale by the time anyone would read it. The lock had quietly
grown from a ban on tuning into a ban on publishing, and nothing in report 02 §5.1
ever asked for the second one.

The owner ruled: 2025 becomes the display season.

## Decision

**Three sentences, and the config now says all three.**

1. **The constants were fixed on 2021-2023 and validated on 2024.** Unchanged by
   this ADR and unchangeable by it: this document moves no constant.
2. **2025 was scored once, on 2026-08-15, and the scorecard is published.** One
   walk-forward pass, `--unlock-holdout`, every gate criterion, every baseline,
   published whatever it said. It said the gate fails. `holdout_seasons` is now
   `[]` and `holdout_scored = [2025]` records that the shot was taken and when.
3. **2026 gets ranked every week and fitted to never.** The ban moves off the
   season and onto the act.

```toml
holdout_seasons = []          # spent
holdout_locked  = false       # nothing is barred from being SCORED
holdout_scored  = [2025]
holdout_scored_on = "2026-08-15"

no_fit_seasons = [2026]       # no fitting path may take a 2026 outcome as its response
no_fit_locked  = true
```

### Why 2026 is not "the new sealed season", and this is the load-bearing paragraph

Because the site publishes a 2026 board **every week** and grades the 2026
Projection against 2026 results **every week**, and a season that is ranked and
graded in public cannot coherently be described as sealed. Anyone reading the
board and then reading the word "holdout" would be right to conclude that one of
the two was untrue.

The thing worth protecting was never the season. It was the act. A holdout bans
**tuning**; it does not ban publishing, and the old block conflated the two
because while a season is sealed the two happen to coincide.

So the guard becomes mechanical and narrow: **no fitting or tuning path may take a
2026 outcome as its response.** Reading 2026, ranking 2026, scoring 2026,
rendering 2026 and grading a frozen prediction against 2026 are all permitted and
all happen weekly. Fitting a coefficient against 2026 outcomes raises
`HoldoutLocked` from `projection.holdout.assert_no_target_is_locked` — the same
exception the backtest raises, deliberately, because it is the same breach.

That guard is not new machinery. It is the machinery ADR 0010 already built,
pointed at a better-named list. `assert_no_target_is_locked` has always checked
the TARGET of a design transition and never the source, precisely because reading
and fitting are different acts; all that changed is that the list it reads is now
called what it always meant.

### The single-shot apparatus stays

`holdout_seasons` and `holdout_locked` keep their meaning and their code path.
`walkforward.HoldoutLocked` still refuses to score any season named there without
`unlock_holdout=True`, and `tests/unit/test_walkforward.py` still proves it
against a synthetic config. The next time a season genuinely needs sealing, one
line turns it back on. Deleting a working guard because it currently has nothing
to guard is how a project loses a capability it will want back.

## What the scorecard said

The gate fails. **One of five decidable criteria passes.**

| criterion | threshold | 2025 | verdict |
|---|---|---:|:---:|
| Straight-up accuracy | ≥ 0.70 | 0.6949 | **FAIL** |
| MAE | ≤ 12.8 | 12.635 | pass |
| RMSE | ≤ 15.8 | 15.997 | **FAIL** |
| Worst-decile calibration | ≤ 5.0 pp | 9.52 pp | **FAIL** |
| Violations at or below every scored system | `all_scored_systems` | 0.1720, loses to `winpct` 0.1671 | **FAIL** |
| Brier beats every baseline | — | — | undecided |
| Retro-vs-live divergence monotone | — | — | undecided |

**The one pass is worth naming plainly.** MAE 12.635 clears the 12.8 ceiling, and
**no tune season ever did**: the same ordering reads 13.038 on 2021-2023. The
first time the margin ceiling was met, it was met on a season the model had never
been shown. That is the only line in the evaluation that reads better than the
seasons the constants were fitted on, and it is exactly the direction a fair
holdout is supposed to be able to produce.

The four failures are the same four that ADR 0009 recorded on the tune seasons.
Calibration is worse on 2025 (9.52 pp) than the tuned figure (7.37 pp), which is
what an out-of-sample reading of a constant fitted for calibration is entitled to
do and is the reason the gate is written against a season rather than against a
search.

### The two undecided criteria are not decided here, on purpose

`brier_beats_all_baselines` and `retro_vs_live_monotone` have been reported as
`null` by `metrics.check_gate` since before the constants were frozen. Neither has
a rule precise enough to evaluate: "baseline" is undefined in `[gate]` (the
competition systems and this project's own lower layers are both in the table),
and "monotone" does not say whether it means strictly, or over the published
window, or up to a tolerance.

Campaign 2 already left the retro-monotone criterion flagged as needing a
pre-registered decision rather than an inherited one, and the same objection
applies to the Brier criterion for the same reason. **Choosing either reading now,
having just seen the holdout, would be selecting a rule against a result** — the
identical failure the holdout existed to prevent, one level up. So the scorecard
publishes the evidence in full and leaves both verdicts where the harness leaves
them:

- **Brier.** The house scores 0.1916 and loses to `l2` (0.1874), `elo` (0.1900),
  `l1` (0.1906) and `srs` (0.1914). Under either reading of "baseline" it loses.
  Nothing is hidden by declining to set the boolean.
- **Monotone.** The curve falls from 7.18 places at `2025-regu-w05` to 0.00 at
  `2025-post-w01` and is **not strictly monotone**: `2025-regu-w06` → `w07` rises
  by 0.16 places.

A successor campaign should pre-register both rules and then decide them, in that
order.

## Consequences

1. **2025 is the site's example season.** A full fixture tree ships: 16 regular
   weeks, live and hindsight surfaces, bootstrap rank intervals, `divergence.json`,
   and the house, `full-merit` and `just-win` lenses under
   [`docs/fixture-contract-recipes.md`](../fixture-contract-recipes.md).
2. **The 2025 methodology pages carry the 2025 gate**, not a tune-season gate.
   That required one real fix: `publish/serving.py` labelled every backtest row
   `tune_<min>_<max>` unconditionally, so the holdout's numbers would have shipped
   labelled `tune_2025_2025`. The harness now writes `protocol.split` from the
   config's own season roles and the site reads it. A false provenance label on a
   published metric is not a cosmetic defect for this project.
3. **The 2025 Projection is publishable, and it is a stronger artifact than the
   2024 grading demo.** `design_transitions` did not move: 2024→2025 was absent
   while 2025 was sealed and is absent now, so applying the shipped recipe to
   2024→2025 is an out-of-sample application of coefficients that were frozen
   before anybody read a 2025 number. The 2024 demo has to *exclude* a transition
   to make its claim; the 2025 one does not.
4. **The provenance survives the unlock.** `holdout.source_season_note` gains a
   third case: a season that WAS the holdout says so, names the date it was
   scored, and points at the scorecard. A 2026 artifact regressing toward 2025
   still tells its reader the whole history instead of going quiet the moment the
   quiet became legal.
5. **The gate still fails, in public, for the fourth consecutive published
   evaluation, on the same four criteria.** Opening the season changes what can be
   shown and changes no verdict. Any page that renders 2025 should link the
   scorecard.
6. **No constant may move on this evidence without a new campaign.** Anyone who
   changes a number in `configs/default.toml` after today has seen the scorecard,
   and the honest way to do it is a pre-registered protocol on a re-designated
   split, announced in public, exactly as ADR 0007 demanded of the second reading
   of 2024. This ADR is that announcement for 2025 and it announces no change.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Leave 2025 sealed and keep 2023 as the display season.** Preserves a promise
  nobody made. The lock protects the tuning, and the tuning is finished and
  frozen. A three-year-old example season is a cost paid for nothing.
- **Call 2026 the new sealed holdout.** Rejected on the merits and not only on the
  copy: the site publishes a 2026 board weekly and grades the 2026 Projection
  against 2026 weekly, so the word would contradict the page it sits on. The ban
  that is actually wanted is on fitting, and that is what is now written down.
- **Score 2025 and keep the result private until it looked better.** The single
  shot means the result is the result. It is published on the same day it was
  read, with the command, the sha, and the date.
- **Wire the two undecided criteria while we were in there, since the numbers were
  already on screen.** That is choosing a rule against a result. See above.
- **Re-tune the four failing criteria now that a fourth season of evidence
  exists.** Every failure is measured on a season that is now visible. A search
  conditioned on it validates against nothing. Pre-register first; that has been
  the answer twice already and it is the answer again.
- **Delete `holdout_seasons` and `holdout_locked` entirely.** They are a working
  guard with a passing test. An empty list is a state; a deleted mechanism is a
  capability nobody notices is gone until they need it.
